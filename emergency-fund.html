Emergency fund: how much, and where to keep it

5 min read · Tidonet Guides

An emergency fund is money set aside for things you did not plan and cannot avoid: a broken phone, a car repair, a dentist bill, a month without work. It is not for holidays or sales. Its only job is to be there when something goes wrong.

Why it comes before almost everything else

Without a buffer, every surprise becomes a loan. The repair goes on a credit card, the card charges interest, and a 400 problem turns into a 600 problem. A small fund breaks that chain. It is often the difference between an annoying week and a debt that lasts a year.

How much is enough?

A common guideline is three to six months of essential costs. Essential means what you must pay to keep life running: housing, food, transport, insurance, minimum debt payments. It does not include eating out or streaming.

That can feel impossibly far away, so it helps to think in three steps:

  • Step one: a starter buffer. Enough to cover one common surprise, such as a repair. For many people that is somewhere around one week of pay.
  • Step two: one month of essential costs.
  • Step three: three to six months. People with irregular income, or others depending on them, often aim for the higher end.

A simple example

Say your essential costs are 1,500 a month. Three months is 4,500.

You saveOne month (1,500)Three months (4,500)
75 a month20 months5 years
150 a month10 months2 years 6 months
300 a month5 months1 year 3 months

It is slow at first, and that is normal. The first step is the one that changes how surprises feel.

The key idea: an emergency fund is not an investment. You are not trying to earn a return. You are buying calm.

Where to keep it

  • Safe. Not in shares or anything that can fall in value the same week you need it.
  • Easy to reach. You should be able to get it within a day or two.
  • Separate. A different account from your everyday spending, so it is not spent by accident.

A plain savings account fits all three for most people.

What counts as an emergency?

Three quick questions help: Is it unexpected? Is it necessary? Is it urgent? If the answer to all three is yes, use the fund without guilt. That is what it is for. Then rebuild it before you go back to other goals.

The Larry test

One of the questions in our Larry or Bella quiz is "Your phone breaks. How do you pay for a new one?" Larry's answer is the credit card, because future Larry can deal with it. Bella's answer is two words: emergency fund.

A fair note

The right size depends on your job, your family and your costs. The figures here are examples for learning, not personal advice.
