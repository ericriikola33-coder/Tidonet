Think in hours, not in price tags

3 min read · Tidonet Guides

A price tag tells you how much money something costs. It does not tell you how much of your life it costs. Converting prices into work hours is one of the simplest ways to make better spending decisions.

How to do it

Take the amount you actually receive per hour after tax. Then divide the price by that number.

Price ÷ take-home pay per hour = hours of work

If you take home 20 an hour, a 1,200 phone costs you 60 hours. That is a week and a half of full-time work.

Why it works

Money can feel abstract, especially when you pay with a card or your phone. Time does not. Everyone knows what a long work day feels like. When you see a purchase as "two days of work", you naturally ask a better question: is this worth it to me?

It is not about never buying things

Sometimes the answer is yes, and that is fine. A good pair of shoes you wear every day might be worth five hours. A subscription you forgot about probably is not worth one. The goal is not to feel guilty. The goal is to choose on purpose.

Quick tips

  • Use your real take-home pay, not your salary before tax.
  • Include commuting time if you want an even more honest number.
  • Try it on monthly costs too. A subscription you pay every month adds up over a year.
