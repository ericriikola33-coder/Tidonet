What inflation does to your cash

4 min read · Tidonet Guides

Inflation means prices rise over time. A coffee, a bus ticket and a month of rent all tend to cost more than they did ten years ago. The other way to say the same thing: each unit of money buys a little less every year.

The number on the note does not change

This is what makes inflation easy to miss. If you put a 100 note in a drawer, it is still a 100 note in thirty years. Nothing was taken from you. But the things you could have bought with it have become more expensive, so the note does less work.

How much does 100 buy later?

Here is what 100 kept as cash can buy, measured in today's prices, at three different inflation rates:

10 years≈ 82≈ 74≈ 61

30 years≈ 55≈ 41≈ 23

At 3% a year, cash loses more than half of its buying power in thirty years. It still says 100. It buys what about 41 buys today.

The key idea: money that does not grow is slowly shrinking. Standing still is not neutral.

Why cash is still useful

None of this means cash is bad. Money you will need soon should be safe and easy to reach, even if inflation nibbles at it. That is the whole point of an emergency fund. Inflation becomes a real problem for money you will not touch for many years.

What people do about it

  • Short-term money often sits in a savings account. The interest rarely beats inflation by much, but it reduces the loss and keeps the money safe.
  • Long-term money is often invested, for example in broad index funds, because over long periods they have historically grown faster than prices. They can also fall, sometimes sharply, so this only fits money you can leave alone for years.
  • Pay rises matter too. If your pay goes up by less than inflation, you can afford less than before, even with a higher number on the payslip.

Larry under the mattress

In our videos, Larry is a one dollar bill who lives under a mattress. Thirty years later he is still exactly one dollar, and he is very pleased about that. Bella spent the same thirty years in an index fund. Larry did not lose money. He just stood still while everything else moved.

A fair note

Inflation is different every year and in every country, and nobody knows future rates. The table uses steady rates to show the pattern, not to predict your prices.
