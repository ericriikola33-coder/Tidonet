Index funds, explained simply

5 min read · Tidonet Guides

An index fund is a simple way to own a tiny piece of many companies at once. Instead of trying to pick which company will do well, the fund buys all the companies on a list, called an index, and holds them.

What is an index?

An index is just a list of companies chosen by a rule, for example the largest few hundred companies in a country, or thousands of companies around the world. News reports that say "the market went up today" are usually talking about an index.

How the fund works

When you put money into an index fund, it is pooled with everyone else's and used to buy every company on the list, in the same proportions as the index. If the companies together become more valuable, your share of the fund does too. If they fall, so does your share. Nobody is making clever guesses. The fund simply follows the list.

Why many people like them

  • Spread risk. One company can fail completely. Hundreds of companies failing at the same time is far less likely.
  • Low cost. Following a list is cheap, so fees are usually much lower than for funds run by managers who pick shares.
  • Simple. There is nothing to research every week. Many people add a fixed amount each month and leave it.

Why fees matter so much

A yearly fee is taken as a percentage of everything you have in the fund, every year. Because of compounding, a small difference becomes large over time. Take 200 a month for 30 years:

Average growth after feesYou put inPossible value
7% a year72,000≈ 244,000
6% a year72,000≈ 201,000

One percentage point less each year means about 43,000 less at the end. Same savings, same effort.

The key idea: you cannot control what the market does. You can control what you pay in fees and how long you stay invested.

The risks, plainly

  • Values go down as well as up. In bad years a broad index can fall by a third or more, and it can take years to recover.
  • No guarantees. Long-term averages describe the past. The future can be different.
  • Timing matters if you need the money soon. Money you will need within a few years usually does not belong in shares at all.

Three questions before you invest

  • Do I have an emergency fund first?
  • Do I have expensive debt that should be paid down before investing?
  • Can I leave this money alone for many years, even if its value drops for a while?

Bella's job

In our videos, Bella is a one dollar bill who "got a job in an index fund". She works every day, takes no coffee breaks and does not complain. It is a joke, but it describes the idea well: the money does the work, slowly, for a long time.

A fair warning

This page explains how index funds work in general. It is not a recommendation to buy any fund. Rules, taxes and available funds differ between countries, and returns are never guaranteed.
