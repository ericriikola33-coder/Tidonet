How to do a subscription audit in 20 minutes

4 min read · Tidonet Guides

Subscriptions are built to be forgotten. The amounts are small, the payments are automatic and cancelling takes more steps than signing up. Most people who check find at least one they did not know they were still paying for.

A subscription audit is a simple check of everything that charges you on repeat. It takes about twenty minutes and usually pays for itself the same day.

Step 1: find them all

  • Bank and card statements. Go through the last two months and write down every payment that repeats.
  • Your phone's subscription list. Both major app stores have a page that shows active subscriptions.
  • Your email. Search for words like "receipt", "renewal" and "subscription".
  • Yearly charges. These are the easiest to miss, because they only show up once. Look back twelve months for them.

Step 2: write down the yearly cost

Multiply each monthly price by twelve. A price of 12.99 feels like nothing. 156 a year is easier to judge. Add them all up. The total surprises almost everyone.

Step 3: keep, pause or cancel

For each one, ask three questions:

  • Did I use it in the last 30 days?
  • Would I sign up again today, at this price?
  • Is there a cheaper plan, or a free version, that would do?

If the answer to the first two is no, cancel it. You can always come back. Services are very happy to let you rejoin.

Step 4: cancel properly

Deleting an app does not cancel the subscription. Cancel inside the service or the app store, and keep the confirmation email. Check your next statement to make sure the charge stopped.

What the forgotten ones cost

Say the audit frees up 50 a month. That is 600 a year in your pocket. If you invested it instead, at an average of 7% a year:

The key idea: the goal is not zero subscriptions. It is paying only for the ones you would choose again.

Keep it clean

  • Put a reminder in your calendar the day before every free trial ends.
  • Repeat the audit twice a year. Prices rise quietly.
  • Rotate instead of stacking: keep one streaming service at a time and switch when you run out of things to watch.

A fair note

The investment column uses a steady 7% average to show the pattern. Real returns vary and are never guaranteed.
