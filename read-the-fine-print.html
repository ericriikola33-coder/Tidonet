Read the fine print: 7 things to check before you sign a loan

5 min read · Tidonet Guides

The big print in a loan offer is written to sell it: a low monthly payment, fast approval, money today. The small print is where the price is. You do not need to be a lawyer to read it. You need to know which seven lines to look for.

1. The APR, not just the interest rate

The annual percentage rate includes the interest and most compulsory fees, expressed as one yearly figure. It is the fairest single number for comparing two loans. If an offer only shows a monthly rate, multiply by twelve to get a rough yearly picture. 2% a month is about 24% a year.

2. The total amount you will repay

This is the most honest line in the document. It adds up every payment over the whole loan. Compare it with the amount you are borrowing. The difference is what the loan costs you.

3. Fees

Look for set-up fees, monthly account fees and charges for paper statements or payment by a certain method. A loan with a low rate and high fees can cost more than one with a higher rate and none.

4. Whether the rate can change

A fixed rate stays the same. A variable rate can go up, and your payment with it. If the rate is variable, ask what your payment would be if the rate rose by two or three percentage points, and whether you could still afford it.

5. The length of the loan

A longer loan means a lower monthly payment and a higher total cost. Stretching a loan to make the monthly figure look comfortable is one of the most common ways people pay far more than they expected.

6. What happens if you pay late

Find the late fee, any penalty interest rate, and how quickly a missed payment is reported or passed to collection. Things go wrong in real life. You should know the cost of one bad month before it happens.

7. What happens if you pay early

Some loans let you repay early for free, which saves interest. Others charge a fee for it. If you might get the money together sooner, this line matters.

The key idea: judge a loan by its total cost, not its monthly payment. A small monthly number can hide a very large total.

Warning signs

  • "Today only" or pressure to sign immediately. A fair offer is still fair tomorrow.
  • Nobody can tell you the total amount you will repay.
  • You are asked to pay a fee before you receive the loan.
  • You are told the details do not matter, or that everybody just signs.

Questions worth asking out loud

  • What is the APR?
  • How much will I have paid in total when the loan is finished?
  • What does it cost if I am a week late?
  • Can I take the agreement home and read it first?

If the answer to the last one is no, that is your answer.

A fair note

Loan rules and required disclosures differ between countries. This page is general education, not legal or financial advice. For a large loan, it is reasonable to ask an independent adviser or a consumer advice service to look at the agreement with you.
